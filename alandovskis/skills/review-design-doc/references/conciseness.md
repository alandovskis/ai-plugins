# Conciseness lens

Question: does every sentence and section earn its place for a reader deciding whether the design is right?

Check the doc for:

- Decisions specified that are cheap to reverse (UI pagination, library choice behind an interface); the doc is writing the implementation.
- Sections included only because a template lists them, with nothing a reader could get wrong without them.
- Content repeated across sections (the objective restated in Background, goals restated as scenarios).
- Background that tells history with no bearing on a decision.
- Alternatives considered written as exhaustive records instead of a few lines and a rejection reason.
- Glossary entries for terms that are common or already defined inline.
- Preamble, hedging and filler that carry no information ("it is worth noting that").
- Detail a link would replace: long code, full API tables or specs that exist elsewhere.
- Lists of equal-weight items where only one or two matter.

Do not remove content that answers "what is the penalty for being wrong?" for an irreversible decision, or any measurable target. Do not report unclear wording (Clarity).
